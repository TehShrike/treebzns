    Design notes

    • The header numbers answer the questions people ask before they pick up the phone: what is open, what is owed, what have they spent, how many jobs, and how often they say yes. Accepted quotes are projects that reached an approved document. Declined quotes are projects that hit a declined document.
    • Tags are made ad hoc while tagging. There is no tag management screen. A tag is a name and a color (client_tag), shared across the company, and it shows anywhere the client shows: this header, the lead search, and the estimating screen.
    • Each invoice edits its tax, discount, and fee in one panel. A discount is one flat amount or one percent, never both, with a required description when it is not zero.
    • The Projects tab reuses the project list table and its columns. Only the filters change. One table, two screens.
    • Contacts and addresses are separate lists. A contact is a person, an address is a place, and a project copies from both at lead time.
    • The declined project stays in the list with its reason. That history is what makes the decline reasons worth collecting.
    • Open: the client screen edits fields in place with no save button in this mockup. Does the app save on blur, or does it need Save and Cancel? Every other screen has the same question.
    • Open: a payment can exist with no invoice (payment.project_id and payment_invoice are both optional). The payments table needs a row shape for "paid, not applied".
    • Open: src/notes/todo.md wants a default filter on the client search so it does not list every client. "Has an open project" is the candidate.
